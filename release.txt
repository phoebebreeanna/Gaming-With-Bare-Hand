# HandMouse v1.0.0 - Windows & macOS (Apple Silicon)

Control your mouse, keyboard, and games using only your hand and a webcam - no extra hardware required. Powered by MediaPipe, OpenCV, PyTorch, and PySide6.

## Gesture Control

- Mouse mode - move the cursor, left/right click, drag, and scroll with hand gestures
- Subway Surfers mode - swipe and tilt gestures mapped to arrow keys and space
- Racing mode - two-hand tilt steering with thumb-up accelerate/brake, plus horn/camera actions
- Open World mode - 18 gestures mapped to movement, abilities, team commands, and more
- Air Hockey mode - bundled local 2-player game; drive your paddle with wrist position, fire powers by finger count
- Custom gesture modes - define and train your own gesture set beyond the built-in modes

## Training & Customization

- In-app model training - collect gesture samples and train your own neural network per mode
- Live model swap - switch between Default and Custom models without restarting
- Train Model Guide - step-by-step walkthrough with reference videos/images for the full training workflow
- Key bindings editor - remap any gesture to any key, with a one-click reset to defaults
- Per-mode gesture lock - lock the in-game gesture that switches modes, so you don't switch by accident mid-play

## Quality of Life

- Live camera feed with real-time hand skeleton overlay and distance alert
- Multi-camera support - scan and switch cameras on demand from Settings
- Zone calibration (Small / Medium / Large movement zone)
- Dark / Light theme across the whole app
- Status overlay - small always-on-top panel that stays visible when you switch to another app or game
- HandBot chatbot - ask questions from the chat panel; runs fully offline on a bundled local model, or switch to ChatGPT with your own API key

## Requirements

- Windows or macOS
- A webcam or external USB camera
- ~2 GB free disk space (bundled ML models and runtime)

## Installation

Download the installer for your platform from the Assets below, run it, and launch HandMouse. On first launch on macOS, grant Camera and Accessibility permission when prompted (System Settings > Privacy & Security) so gesture-to-input mapping works correctly.
