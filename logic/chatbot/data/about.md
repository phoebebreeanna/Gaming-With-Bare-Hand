# About HANDMOUSE

HANDMOUSE ("Gaming with Bare Hands") lets you play PC games using hand
gestures captured by your webcam, instead of — or alongside — a keyboard and
mouse. Show your hand to the camera and the app translates your gestures into
game controls in real time.

The HANDMOUSE website is at https://handmouse.vercel.app

## Cost

HANDMOUSE is completely free to use right now. There's no account to create,
no payment, and no subscription — everything runs on your own computer.

## What to expect (performance)

Under normal conditions (good lighting, your hand clearly visible, sitting at
the right distance from the camera), you can expect:
- Around 15–30 frames per second of smooth tracking.
- About 100–200 milliseconds between making a gesture and seeing the action
  happen — fast enough to feel responsive, though not as instant as a
  physical keyboard or mouse.
- Roughly 85% or better gesture recognition accuracy.

If performance feels off, check the Troubleshooting section of the User
Guide — lighting, distance, and background clutter are the biggest factors.

## Who it's for

Casual PC gamers, roughly aged 13–35, who want a more physical and fun way to
play — especially fans of racing games, endless runners, and open-world
action games. It's not designed for competitive or professional gamers who
need instant, highly precise input, or for people who need a full
accessibility solution for significant motor impairments.

## Supported game modes

- **Mouse Mode** — full cursor control with your hand: move, click,
  right-click, drag, and scroll.
- **Subway Surfers Mode** — jump, roll, move left/right, and a space-bar
  action, tuned for endless runner games.
- **Racing Mode** — steer, accelerate, and brake using both hands.
- **Open World Mode** — a larger gesture set for action/open-world games like
  Wuthering Waves or Minecraft.

You can switch between these anytime from the Settings page, or mid-game with
a hold gesture — see the User Guide for details.

## Privacy

Everything runs locally on your computer. Your webcam video is only
processed in memory to detect your hand position — it is never saved to
disk or sent anywhere over the internet.

The one exception is if you choose to train custom gestures: your recorded
samples and the resulting custom model are saved locally on your device so
you can reuse them. This only happens if you use the Train Model feature —
it's entirely optional, and nothing is ever sent off your computer.
